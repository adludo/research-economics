**Research question:** Can an hour of leisure be assigned a consumption-money value? **Yes, conditionally:** utility-based shadow values, an interior net-wage condition, and consumption-equivalent comparisons provide different calculations. The [equation guide and worked examples](/findings/leisure-value/) explain which question each answers.

This is a focused literature search, not a systematic review. One new primary paper was directly read from a pinned author-hosted published copy. The earlier foundations below are identified separately; the new reading does not certify their existing wiki summaries.

## Brouillette, Jones, and Klenow (2025): an explicit leisure valuation

**Citation:** Jean-Félix Brouillette, Charles I. Jones, and Peter J. Klenow. “Race and Economic Well-Being in the United States.” *AER: Insights* 7(4), 429–446.

**Links:** [DOI / publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aeri.20240467) · [Inspected author PDF](https://github.com/jfbrou/jfbrou.github.io/blob/1eeeb63a3a020e97778eb926cc09ca3ea472d32b/papers/dignity.pdf) · [Retrieval URL, SHA-256 and locators](/data/leisure-value/sources.json).

**Question and method:** The paper builds consumption-equivalent welfare comparisons for Black and White Americans over 1984–2022, accounting for consumption, leisure, inequality, survival and incarceration. This is a period cross-section welfare-accounting framework, not a causal estimate of the effect of another leisure hour.

**Relevant findings and equations:**

- **Explicit preferences:** Flow utility is `constant + ln(c) + v(ℓ)`, with `v(ℓ)=−θε/(1+ε) × (1−ℓ)^((1+ε)/ε)`. This gives an explicit way to trade leisure against consumption. **Printed p.433; PDF p.5; unnumbered utility equations.**
- **Wage calibration:** The static first-order condition equates `u_ℓ/u_c` to a consistently measured after-tax wage. The authors use it to calibrate the leisure weight. It is a modelling condition, not evidence of one observed market price of leisure. **Printed p.439; PDF p.11; Section III.**
- **Money-equivalent welfare:** Equation (1) chooses a consumption multiplier that equates welfare across allocations; equation (3) decomposes the wider welfare comparison. Leisure can therefore matter even when it is absent from GDP. **Printed pp.433–434; PDF pp.5–6.**
- **Our derived hourly example:** The stated rounded `C=$31,046`, `ℓ=0.70`, `θ=8.9`, `ε=1`, and a 5,840-hour year imply approximately **$14.19/hour in 2012 dollars**, after converting leisure shares into hours. That monetary result is our derivation, not a reported universal price in the paper. **Calibration pp.439–440; PDF pp.11–12.**

**Measurement and interpretation limits:** “Leisure” is broad non-market waking time derived from work hours, with household and unemployment adjustments, rather than diary recreation alone (**pp.438–439**). The preference weight is calibrated, the baseline elasticity is assumed, and common preference parameters do not imply identical marginal money values for every individual. The hourly derivation holds other welfare dimensions fixed; it does not reproduce the full lifetime accounting exercise.

**Reading status:** Masthead metadata, the equations and the stated passages were inspected in the author-hosted publication copy. Independent publisher/DOI authentication, corrections/retractions, underlying data and full empirical replication remain unverified. No funding or disclosure claim is inferred.

## Jones and Klenow (2016): the earlier welfare framework

**Citation:** Charles I. Jones and Peter J. Klenow. “Beyond GDP? Welfare across Countries and Time.” *American Economic Review* 106(9), 2426–2457. [Publisher reference](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20110236) · [Author-copy access route](https://web.stanford.edu/~chadj/JonesKlenowAER2016.pdf) · [Existing wiki entry](/papers/leisure-productivity/#jones-and-klenow-2016-valuing-leisure-beyond-gdp).

**Why read it:** It is the earlier consumption-equivalent welfare framework referenced by the 2025 paper. That bibliographic reference was checked in the inspected copy's **printed p.446; PDF p.18**. It is a useful route for understanding why leisure enters welfare alongside consumption and other dimensions.

**Status boundary:** The original 2016 full text and publisher record were blocked in this session. The explicit equations and numerical example on the new guide are grounded in the read 2025 source, not presented as newly verified claims from the unread 2016 article.

## Becker (1965): the opportunity-cost foundation

**Citation:** Gary S. Becker. “A Theory of the Allocation of Time.” *The Economic Journal* 75(299), 493–517. [DOI / journal reference](https://doi.org/10.2307/2228949) · [Existing wiki entry](/papers/leisure/#becker-1965-the-opportunity-cost-of-time).

**Why read it:** This is the wiki's established theoretical starting point for the opportunity cost of time. Keep that opportunity cost distinct from a universal monetary value of enjoyment.

**Status boundary:** The original full text and publisher record were not accessible here. No new numerical result or verified equation from Becker is asserted by this addition. The general marginal-rate-of-substitution explanation is linked to the condition directly inspected in the 2025 paper.

## What this literature supports

There are usable valuation equations, but they require preferences, a wage-based calibration or other choice evidence. A rough wage benchmark, a local shadow value and a finite willingness-to-pay amount are different objects. Time-use hours alone cannot determine all of them, and none supplies a context-free societal price for every leisure hour.

## 3. Aguiar and Hurst (2007): five decades of leisure trends

- Attempted primary reference: https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.122.3.969.
- Retrieval result: proxy tunnel 403; no metadata or full text read.

Claim set requiring primary-source checking:

- **Key findings:** Weekly leisure increased by approximately **6–9 hours for men** and **4–8 hours for women**, depending on the definition. Men's gains largely reflected less paid work; women's gains largely reflected less household production. Gains increasingly favoured less-educated adults.

Claim set requiring primary-source checking:

- **Key finding:** The output–hours relationship is nonlinear: beyond long schedules, additional hours produce diminishing output gains. Shorter schedules can therefore raise output per hour without proportionate losses of total output.
